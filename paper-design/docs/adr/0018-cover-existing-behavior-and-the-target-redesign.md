# Cover existing behavior and the target redesign

A release completed its business-discrepancy tickets while the shared sidebar and screen layouts still used the old design. Checking stories against their linked artboards could establish that an action worked. It could also miss a surface that had never received a ticket.

For brownfield redesigns, accepted behavior and the target composition therefore have independent inventories and independent evidence. The target owns the new visual language. Confirmed behavior missing from it becomes design work. A named existing product boundary can supply the evidence for drawing before a migration Spec exists. Once published, the Spec holds or links the coverage record and the UI tickets point implementers to their particular designs. This extends the authority, scope and tracker conventions in ADRs 0001, 0004 and 0015. The approval and Relay boundaries in ADRs 0016 and 0017 still apply.

A partial reference can also guide a surrounding flow. The user's intent determines its reach. Drawing costly alternatives first makes them reviewable. Implementing a predictable inferred surface first can save duplicate work. The inventory records the resulting code-to-Paper drift until the surface has been synchronized and compared.
